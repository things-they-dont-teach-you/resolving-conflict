# Resolving conflict and communicating clearly

How to handle disagreements without making them worse, taught by your AI assistant, which plays
the other person so you can practise: listening, saying hard things without blame, finding middle
ground, asking for a raise, refund or repair, and knowing when to set a boundary or walk away.

> [!WARNING]
> **Under development. Do not rely on this course yet.** The lessons have not been reviewed by a
> qualified mediator or counsellor and may contain mistakes. For anything serious, talk to a
> qualified counsellor, mediator or adviser.
>
> **Are you a mediator, counsellor or psychologist?** We're looking for professionals to review
> these lessons.
> [Offer to review](https://github.com/things-they-dont-teach-you/resolving-conflict/issues/new?template=volunteer-reviewer.yml)
> or use the [contact form](https://things-they-dont-teach-you.github.io/#contact).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Abuse is not a conflict to resolve.** If someone hurts, threatens or controls you, or you are
> afraid of them, better communication is not the answer. Help lines are in
> [facts.md](facts.md#emergency-numbers). If you are in danger now, call your emergency number.

## This is not therapy or legal advice

This course teaches communication skills. It is not counselling, mediation or legal advice. For a
conflict that is affecting your health, see a counsellor; for a dispute with legal stakes, such as
at work or with a landlord, see an adviser in your country.

## Known gaps

- **Not yet reviewed** by a mediator, counsellor or psychologist.
- **Help lines cover three countries only:** South Africa, the UK and the US. Learners elsewhere
  are told to find their local emergency number and abuse service.
- **UK nations:** GOV.UK lists separate helplines for Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland. They
  are left out until each is checked on the provider's own site.
- **South Africa:** only the police (10111) and the GBV Command Centre are listed. Other services
  (for example 112 from mobiles, and NGO helplines) were not verified and are left out. The
  centre's 24-hour opening comes from government news (SAnews), not the centre's own page.
- **Workplace steps** follow Acas, a UK body; they are offered as a general model. The legal side
  for South Africa is meant for the `south-african-rights-at-work` course, which doesn't exist yet.
- **Consumer complaints:** the steps to escalate a refund differ by country and are not detailed;
  `facts.md` links one official body each for South Africa, the UK and the US.
- **Culture:** most of the research behind these techniques comes from the US. How direct it is
  polite to be differs between cultures and families, and no source here covers that.
- **Conflict styles:** the Thomas-Kilmann questionnaire is a paid product; the course only
  describes the five styles, and does not test learners.
- **Not used:** a widely repeated claim that the first minutes of an argument predict its outcome
  in a fixed percentage of cases could not be checked against the original study.
