## What counts as abuse

Source: [WHO: Violence against women fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/violence-against-women),
checked 2026-11-11. WHO describes intimate partner violence as behaviour by a partner or
ex-partner that causes physical, sexual or psychological harm, including physical aggression,
sexual coercion, psychological abuse and controlling behaviours. Anyone can experience it.

Source: [love is respect: What are my boundaries?](https://www.loveisrespect.org/resources/what-are-my-boundaries/)
(National Domestic Violence Hotline), checked 2026-11-11. Warning signs include someone who reacts
with anger or violence when you set a boundary, controls who you see, or pressures you into
dropping your boundaries.

## Escalation and the four horsemen

Source: [The Gottman Institute: The Four Horsemen](https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-four-horsemen-recognizing-criticism-contempt-defensiveness-and-stonewalling/)
and [The Four Horsemen: The Antidotes](https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-four-horsemen-the-antidotes/),
from John Gottman's research on couples, checked 2026-11-11.

| Pattern | What it looks like | Antidote |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Criticism | Attacking the person's character, not raising a specific complaint | Complain about the behaviour, with "I" statements about your feelings and needs |
| Contempt | Mocking, name-calling, eye-rolling | Build a habit of appreciation and respect |
| Defensiveness | Excuses, blaming back | Accept your share of responsibility |
| Stonewalling | Shutting down, going silent | Call a break and calm down for at least 20 minutes, then come back |

Stonewalling often comes from being "flooded": the body is so stressed that clear thinking stops.

## Conflict styles

Source: [Kilmann Diagnostics: Using the Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Mode Instrument](https://kilmanndiagnostics.com/using-the-thomas-kilmann-conflict-mode-instrument/),
checked 2026-11-11. Five ways of handling conflict, set by how assertive (pursuing your own
concerns) and how cooperative (meeting the other person's) you are: competing, collaborating,
compromising, avoiding and accommodating. Which one fits depends on the situation. The instrument
itself is a paid questionnaire; the course only describes the five styles.

## Active listening

Source: [University of Iowa, Conflict Management: Active listening](https://conflictmanagement.org.uiowa.edu/active-listening),
checked 2026-11-11. Six skills: encourage, question, restate (the facts, in your own words),
reflect (the feelings), summarise, and validate.

## I-statements

Source: [University of Iowa, Conflict Management: I statements](https://conflictmanagement.org.uiowa.edu/i-statements),
checked 2026-11-11. Say how you feel, describe the situation neutrally, say how it affects you,
then say what you'd like to happen.

## Principled negotiation

Source: [Harvard Program on Negotiation: Principled negotiation](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/principled-negotiation-focus-interests-create-value/),
from Fisher, Ury and Patton's *Getting to Yes*, checked 2026-11-11. Four principles: separate the
people from the problem; focus on interests, not positions; invent options for mutual gain; insist
on objective criteria.

Source: [Harvard Program on Negotiation: What is BATNA?](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/batna/translate-your-batna-to-the-current-deal/),
checked 2026-11-11. Your BATNA (best alternative to a negotiated agreement) is what you'll do if
you can't agree. Knowing it tells you when to walk away.

## Asking for a raise

Source: [Harvard Program on Negotiation: How to ask for a raise](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/salary-negotiations/how-to-ask-for-a-salary-increase/),
checked 2026-11-11. Do your homework (what similar jobs pay, how the employer is doing); look at
the whole package, not just pay; frame the request around the value you bring, not your own bills.

## Complaints and refunds

Start with the seller or company's own complaints process; go to an official body only if that
fails. Examples, checked 2026-11-11:

- South Africa: [National Consumer Commission](https://thencc.org.za/)
- United Kingdom: [Citizens Advice: consumer](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/)
- United States: [USAGov: consumer complaints](https://www.usa.gov/consumer-complaints)

## Raising a problem at work

Source: [Acas: How to raise a problem at work](https://www.acas.org.uk/how-to-raise-a-problem-at-work),
checked 2026-11-11. Acas is the UK's workplace advice and conciliation service; the steps are a
good model anywhere, but the law differs by country. Check your workplace's policy; raise it
informally first; get support (HR, a union representative); try mediation for relationship
problems; raise a formal grievance if informal steps fail or it's too serious.
