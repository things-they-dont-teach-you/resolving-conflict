# Lesson 5: Asking for things: a raise, a refund, a repair
<!-- facts: Asking for a raise; Complaints and refunds; Principled negotiation -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Prepare for an ask: what they want, why it's fair, and what they'll do if the answer is no.
- Make the ask clearly, in one or two sentences, without apologising for it.
- Put it in writing, and know the next step up if it's refused.
- Handle a "no" or a "not now" calmly.

## Key ideas

**Prepare three things.** What exactly you want (a number, a date, a fix). Why it's fair (your
evidence). And your walk-away option from lesson 4, so you know how far to push.

**Ask clearly, then stop talking.** "I'd like a refund for this kettle; it stopped working after
a week." No long apology, no threat. Ask the person who can actually decide, at a calm time.

**Get it in writing.** After a phone call or chat, send a short message: what you asked, what they
said, and by when. Keep receipts, photos and dates. If you have to take it further, this is your
case.

**Each kind of ask:**

- **A refund or exchange:** start with the shop or company's own complaints process. If that
  fails, go to an official consumer body in your country (examples in `facts.md`).
- **A repair from a landlord:** report it in writing with photos and the date, ask for a fix by a
  reasonable date, and follow up in writing. What a landlord must fix depends on your lease and
  local law, so check those before you insist.
- **A raise:** do your homework on what similar jobs pay, frame it around the value you bring, not
  your bills, and consider the whole package (training, hours, leave), not just pay (`facts.md`).
  Ask for a meeting just for this.

**If the answer is no,** ask what would need to change, and when you can raise it again. A "not
now" with a date is progress.

## Worked example

Lerato's ceiling has a leak. She sends her landlord photos and a message: "The ceiling in the
bedroom has been leaking since Monday, photos attached. Please could it be fixed by Friday?" No
reply, so she follows up two days later, referring to the first message. The repair is booked.

## Role-play

The learner chooses: a manager (asking for a raise), a shop assistant (refund for something
faulty) or a landlord (a repair). The tutor is polite but resistant at first: "That's not our
policy" or "Budgets are tight." The learner states the ask, gives the reason, handles the
pushback, and agrees a next step. Afterwards, they draft the follow-up message.
